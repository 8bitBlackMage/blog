Before I get into the (vegan) meat of this, this post is gonna touch briefly on some heavy mental health stuff, so trigger warning for discussion of sucidality and mental health. 


I have, until fairly recently, considered myself a software developer for life. I always found joy in learning how algorithms worked, new design patterns, libraries and languages, fuck I even tried learning C and Assembly Language just for the challenge. But as I sit in the last months of the big 2k 26, I think I’m ready to call time on this, at least for the foreseeable future. 

# Some backstory

Writing code has been a significant part of my daily life for pretty much all of the last ten years. I learned C++ as prep for my undergraduate degree, a multidisciplinary mix of sound design, composition and software development with a focus on real time audio. From the start of that degree in September 2017 right up until finishing my degree locked down in the height of the 2020 pandemic, I don’t think that an IDE was ever not open with either coursework or a personal project sitting there waiting to be worked on.  

 Before I finished my degree I’d managed to secure employment with a small vendor of audio plugins who shall remain nameless, (so if you’re ever being badgered by your academic tutor to find an in industry placement, sorry — I think I and the rest of my cohort has contributed to unrealistic expectations). Despite a rocky start due to health complications, I think this time was the most fun I’d had working on software projects, I was very proud of the work I did there, and still am to some degree, and a good deal of the plugins I contributed code to still sit in my VST folder — and occasionally my a mix bus. 

Sadly, things cannot stay good forever, and its time to introduce one of the two villains of this story, ChatGPT. Gen AI (or LLM’s for you pedants at home)  made a brief appearance while at my undergrad, a really early transformer model was shown off in my algorithmic composition unit as a tool that was able to churn out interesting facsimiles of pieces it was trained on, but in the intervening five or so years, it had grown and mutated into some strange series of chatbots and agents that could now, if prodded in the right way, produce interesting facsimiles of good code and some of my coworkers were relying it on it more and more.

 At the same time serious discussion was being had across the water in the USA about the risks it posed to creative work in Hollywood, which led to the massive SAG-AFRA strikes. Strikes that had a massive and often ignored crunch in the world of post production, leading to my employer needing to shitcan a good number of its employees, myself included. (As a footnote here, I hold absolutely zero ill will to the guild members whatsoever. Seriously kids, join a union, an injury to one is an injury to all — as we can see here.) 

In retrospect, this layoff did some significant damage to my mental health and if I’d had the opportunity I think some time to actually recover would have been just great. Alas, I had bills to pay, so onto another job I go. This took the form of moving  cross country to the middle of nowhere and work for yet another company who shall remain nameless — mainly because I know that the UK’s libel laws would not be on my side if I named them.

Working for this company was pretty similar to the last place, I wrote code that made sound, I reviewed other peoples code, I attended mindless meetings where non technical people asked for impossible things. The one big change I’d noticed was how much more AI was being used, my coworkers generated entire pull requests with Agents, all the assets were made with AI, all of the requirements were generated by a chatbot. Something about that was pretty soul crushing I gotta tell you. 

With this, we meet our second villain, my own mental health. The combination of the hit to my mental health from the layoffs, a lot of workplace bullying and harassment, and having functionally zero support network in this new and unwelcoming town. I got sick and I mean really sick. Without sugarcoating it, I tried to take my life more than once in the 5 months and change I was stuck there. Unsurprisingly, once a bit more lucid I realised that I needed to not be a part of this life whatsoever, so I ran home to family with my tail in between my legs. 

I stayed with family for a while, entered therapy to process the various damage I’d picked up over the twenty five odd years of my life — but is probably best saved for another post. At this point all hell had broken loose in the world of software engineering and the number of jobs that I managed to get interviews for could be counted on one hand (though as an aside and a small gloat, I was very nearly a senior developer for Mozilla on Firefox, that would have been cool, I can’t lie). I resolved to go and hide in academia for the year, to try and wait it out. So, in September I started a Masters in computer science and after a week I had an interesting revelation; I hated it. 

 The signs had been there before. I didn’t even have an IDE installed on my computer in the months leading up to it, I’d left most of the programming related groups on reddit and other social media. It felt like every avenue of my other major interest, music production, seems to be getting ever further saturated by AI generated shlock. The most recent trend of this being people using LLM’s to generate new custom firmware for synthesisers and other digital music hardware, a gold rush that flooded all of the music production forums with millions of near identical looking copies.

# And breathe..

All in, the net result was much the same as with my last job, I got sick again and was swiftly signed off. Which takes me to now, dropping out of university and hoping to not have to look at a code editor again for a long time. I guess now I try and go all in on the music performance and production career I wanted to do anyway, this has been a fun ten year diversion, right??
